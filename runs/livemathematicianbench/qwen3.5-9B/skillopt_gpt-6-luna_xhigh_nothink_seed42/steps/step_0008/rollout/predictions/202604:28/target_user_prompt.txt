## Question
Let d>0 be fixed and let p>\frac{2d}{1+2d}. For all sufficiently large n for which a dn-regular graph exists, let G_d be a dn-regular graph on n vertices, and let G(n,p) denote the binomial random graph on the same vertex set, where each edge is included independently with probability p. Write G=G_d\cup G(n,p). A triangle packing in G means a collection of edge-disjoint triangles of G. Which statement is the strongest existence claim that holds in this setting, with high probability (that is, with probability tending to 1 as n\to\infty)?

## Choices
A. With high probability, there exists a triangle packing in G whose triangles cover all but O(n) edges of G.
B. With high probability, there exists a triangle packing in G whose triangles cover all but o(n^2) edges of G.
C. With high probability, there exists a triangle packing in G that covers every edge of G(n,p) and all but o(n^2) edges of G_d.
D. With high probability, there exists a triangle packing in G whose triangles cover all but at most 0.01n^2 edges of G.
E. With high probability, there exists a triangle packing in G whose triangles cover all but o(n^2) edges of G, and all uncovered edges lie inside G[X] for some set X of o(n) vertices.
