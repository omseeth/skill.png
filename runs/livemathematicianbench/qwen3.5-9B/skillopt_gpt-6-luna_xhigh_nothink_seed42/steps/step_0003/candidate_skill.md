## Verify Mathematical Multiple-Choice Claims

### Translate definitions and hypotheses literally
Before comparing choices, translate each condition and operation exactly, using all stated domain restrictions. Track conventions such as endpoint inclusion, distinguished points, factors introduced by an operation, divisibility direction, and distinctions such as a field versus its ring of integers. Check congruences, ranges, and membership conditions in equivalent forms; do not silently replace the stated formula with a more natural-seeming one. Test relevant edge cases.

### Compare the full strength of each choice
Split each option into its separate claims and inspect its quantifiers, dependencies, and modifiers: for example, existence versus uniqueness, full realization versus realization modulo torsion, a bound versus a sharper bound, uniformity, parameter dependence, a particular dimension, or an asserted converse that all listed cases occur. Compare the options by logical implication. Choose the strongest statement that is actually established and relevant; do not promote an unsupported refinement or retreat to a weaker claim when a stronger one is proved. If options are equivalent under the hypotheses, do not invent a distinction or break the tie by calling one more standard.

### Verify the theorem, equivalence, and quantitative claims
Identify the precise result being tested and check that its hypotheses imply the stated conclusion. For an equivalence or classification, verify necessity and sufficiency; distinguish a complete classification from a necessary condition, and distinguish related but nonidentical principles or subspaces. For extremal or quantitative choices, order the candidate bounds but independently verify which ones hold, including any named constants, coefficients, lower bounds, extra qualitative properties, and claimed dependence of constants. A sharper-looking estimate or plausible converse is not established by intuition, vague recollection, or association with a familiar theorem.

### Check that the model and regularity assumptions apply
Do not transfer conclusions from a scalar model to a system without checking signs, couplings, and conserved quantities, or reject a claim using an unproved dimensional or blow-up heuristic. For PDE regularity claims, identify the applicable weak equation and verify that the coefficient, lower-order-term, and data assumptions meet the theorem's hypotheses. In particular, do not infer Hölder continuity or higher Sobolev regularity merely from a label such as VMO when the conclusion requires additional conditions.

### Preserve probability and asymptotic qualifiers
When parsing quantifiers, preserve distinctions such as almost surely versus with positive probability, and identify the measure intended by “almost every.” Apply asymptotic definitions and limit statements literally: retain endpoint and strictness details, the order and type of limits (including limsup and iterated limits), and distinctions such as local versus global control. A liminf growth-exponent bound need not imply a uniform estimate with a fixed constant at the endpoint exponent.

For close or compound options, check every clause, including coefficients, exponents, parity restrictions, exceptions, and each item in a list of claimed cases or failures.
