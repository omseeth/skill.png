## Track Witness Dependencies
When options differ in quantifier order, rewrite the quantifiers and note what each existential witness may depend on. In particular, distinguish one fixed choice required to work for every target from a choice made separately after each target is specified.

## Translate Quantitative Guarantees Before Ranking
Put quantitative candidates into standard form before deciding which is strongest. For example, distinguish an asymptotic equivalence from an upper bound, compare decay exponents, separate a parameter-dependent exponent of the input size from a parameter factor multiplying a fixed polynomial, and preserve the order of nested limits and cutoffs.

## Prove Each Added Clause
When choices share a core claim but one adds an exception, scope, or structure, isolate that added clause and verify it independently. Apply an identity across its stated domain; add a restriction or correction term only when a derivation or applicable theorem establishes it. A result for two colors does not by itself extend to every finite coloring; spectral equality giving an abstract graph isomorphism does not give an isomorphism of covers; and an invariant map does not automatically have orbit fibers. Likewise, state only parameter-dependence actually established by the theorem. Do not infer a classification or stronger conclusion from plausibility or from an option's extra specificity.

## Independently Check Algebra and Conventions
Before proving or rejecting a universal formula, copy its expression with its exact grouping, signs, factors, and denominator, then substitute a simple admissible example as a sanity check. For operations described in words, derive the resulting algebraic object from the stated convention before solving for divisibility or equality; do not assume whether a named operation contributes a factor of two.
