## Question
Let $G$ be an undirected graph with $n$ vertices and $m$ edges, and let $u$ and $v$ be two specified vertices of $G$. What is the strongest proved algorithmic statement about deterministically finding an odd cycle that passes through both $u$ and $v$?

## Choices
A. There exists a deterministic algorithm that, given $G$, $u$, and $v$, decides whether an odd cycle through the two specified vertices exists and, if it does, outputs one in polynomial time.
B. There exists a deterministic algorithm that, given $G$, $u$, and $v$, in $\mathrm{O}(n+m)$ time either outputs two cycles through the two specified vertices with opposite parities or correctly reports that no cycle through them exists.
C. There exists a deterministic algorithm that, given $G$, $u$, and $v$, in $\mathrm{O}(n+m)$ time either outputs two odd cycles through the two specified vertices or correctly reports that every cycle through them is even.
D. There exists a deterministic algorithm that, given $G$, $u$, and $v$, finds an odd cycle through the two specified vertices in $\mathrm{O}(n+m)$ time whenever $u$ and $v$ lie in the same biconnected component of $G$.
E. There exists a deterministic algorithm that, given $G$, $u$, and $v$, finds an odd cycle through the two specified vertices in $\mathrm{O}(n+m)$ time.
