## Audit Every Claim and Its Scope
Translate each option into its individual claims, tracking quantifiers, parameter ranges, and the specified domain. Treat words such as “every,” “some,” “only,” “if,” “if and only if,” and “conversely” as substantive; distinguish necessary from sufficient conditions. Verify every clause independently: one unsupported extra clause makes the whole option false. Do not silently add hypotheses or enlarge the stated domain.

## Choose the Strongest Established Claim
First determine which options are supported; only then compare their strength by implication. Choose the strongest option whose full statement is established—not merely the most detailed or plausible one. Preserve qualifiers: for example, “locally” is not “globally,” and a bound uniform in a variable need not be independent of it. Do not infer extra strength from intuition or retreat to a weaker claim based on a speculative obstruction.

## Check Definitions, Hypotheses, and Theorem-Based Bounds
Apply all stated hypotheses and definitions before invoking a theorem or characterization. Do not treat a specialized object as its classical analogue unless the definitions and theorem hypotheses justify it. For quantitative claims, check the exact quantity, inequality direction, parameter range, and whether the bound is uniform or case-specific. Do not repair a remembered theorem with an unestablished exception or structural condition, or rely on vague bounds or invented citations.

## Compare Formulations Literally
Identify exact differences between candidates, including constants, endpoints, normalization, denominator shifts, indexing or count conventions, and strict versus non-strict inequalities. Normalize congruences, range restrictions, divisibility claims, or structural descriptions using the given assumptions; distinguish, for example, restrictions on element orders from restrictions on factor counts or total group order. If two choices reduce to equivalent conditions, do not reject both for that reason—select the formulation best supported by the question.
