## Verify Exact Algebraic Identities
For competing formulas in a quotient ring, use the stated relations to reduce or verify the full expression. Admissible specializations can quickly rule out a sign or factor, but agreement at a few sample points does not prove an identity.

## Track Dependencies and Verify Witnesses and Algorithms
Before evaluating an option, record who chooses each object and what it may depend on. Distinguish one witness required to work for every parameter from a witness chosen separately for each parameter, a constant allowed to depend on ε from one uniform in ε, and every N from all sufficiently large N. Compare formal claims by logical implication or set containment. In algebra-containment claims, check the actual ambient structure and permitted generators: containment in a finitely generated subalgebra is more restrictive than containment in a larger algebra, while restricting the permitted generators makes an algebra-containment claim stronger. For existential claims, construct a witness satisfying every stated condition and calculate the quantity at issue. For algorithmic claims, compare the full input domain, required outputs including failure reports, and running-time bound; a guarantee on a restricted case does not establish the same guarantee on all cases.

## Audit the Exact Difference Between Choices
For near-identical options, rewrite each as its hypotheses and conclusion, then isolate the precise clause that differs: signs or factors, coefficients or powers, index ranges, endpoint requirements, or added conditions and conclusions. Verify that clause independently; a familiar theorem, plausible generalization, or associated method does not establish an extra claim, and a weakened hypothesis requires proof that the conclusion still follows. “Strongest” means the most informative statement that is true—not simply the option with the most detail—and incomparable claims should not be ranked by a vague notion of strength.
