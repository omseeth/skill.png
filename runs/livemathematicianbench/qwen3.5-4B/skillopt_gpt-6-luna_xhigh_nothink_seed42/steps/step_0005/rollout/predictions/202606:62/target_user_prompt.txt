## Question
Let $(D,X)$ be a Dyer system, meaning a group $D$ together with a distinguished generating set $X$ coming from a Dyer graph. For any subset $Y\subseteq X$, let $D_Y$ denote the standard parabolic subgroup generated by $Y$; similarly for $Y'\subseteq X$. Write $\alpha Y\alpha^{-1}:=\{\alpha y\alpha^{-1}:y\in Y\}$, and let $G$ be the graph on subsets of $X$ considered in this setting. Which strongest conclusion holds about the relations among setwise conjugacy of $Y$ and $Y'$ in $D$, conjugacy of the associated subgroups $D_Y$ and $D_{Y'}$, and connectivity in $G$?

## Choices
A. The following are equivalent: (i) there exists \(\alpha\in D\) such that \(\alpha Y\alpha^{-1}=Y'\), (ii) there exists \(\beta\in D\) such that \(\beta D_Y\beta^{-1}=D_{Y'}\), and (iii) \(Y\) and \(Y'\) lie in the same connected component of \(G\). Moreover, there is an algorithm that, whenever these conditions hold, produces explicit elements \(\alpha,\beta\in D\) witnessing (i) and (ii).
B. The following are equivalent: (i) there exists \(\alpha\in D_{Y\cup Y'}\) such that \(\alpha Y\alpha^{-1}=Y'\), (ii) there exists \(\beta\in D\) such that \(\beta D_Y\beta^{-1}=D_{Y'}\), and (iii) \(Y\) and \(Y'\) lie in the same connected component of \(G\). Moreover, there is an algorithm that decides whether these equivalent conditions hold.
C. The following are equivalent: (i) there exists \(\alpha\in D\) such that \(\alpha Y\alpha^{-1}\subseteq Y'\), (ii) there exists \(\beta\in D\) such that \(\beta D_Y\beta^{-1}=D_{Y'}\), and (iii) \(Y\) and \(Y'\) lie in the same connected component of \(G\). Moreover, there is an algorithm that decides whether these equivalent conditions hold.
D. The following are equivalent: (i) there exists $\alpha\in D$ such that $\alpha Y\alpha^{-1}=Y'$, (ii) there exists $\beta\in D$ such that $\beta D_Y\beta^{-1}=D_{Y'}$, and (iii) $Y$ and $Y'$ lie in the same connected component of $G$. Moreover, there is an algorithm that decides whether these equivalent conditions hold.
E. If there exists \(\alpha\in D\) such that \(\alpha Y\alpha^{-1}=Y'\), then there exists \(\beta\in D\) such that \(\beta D_Y\beta^{-1}=D_{Y'}\); likewise, if \(Y\) and \(Y'\) lie in the same connected component of \(G\), then \(D_Y\) and \(D_{Y'}\) are conjugate in \(D\). Moreover, there is an algorithm that decides whether \(D_Y\) and \(D_{Y'}\) are conjugate.
