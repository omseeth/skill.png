## Question
Let $G$ be a finite simple connected graph on at least two vertices, and let $\mu(G)$ denote the spectral radius of its adjacency matrix. For each vertex $v_i$, let $d_i$ be its degree and let
\[
m_i=\frac{1}{d_i}\sum_{v_j\sim v_i} d_j,
\]
where $v_j\sim v_i$ means that $v_i$ and $v_j$ are adjacent. For a vertex-type formula $f(d_i,m_i)$, interpret the numbered candidate bound as $\mu(G)\le \max_i f(d_i,m_i)$; for an edge-type formula $f(d_i,m_i,d_j,m_j)$, interpret it as $\mu(G)\le \max_{v_iv_j\in E(G)} f(d_i,m_i,d_j,m_j)$. Consider the following numbered candidates: vertex-type bounds 1 = $\sqrt{4d_i^3/m_i}$, 4 = $2d_i^2/m_i$, 5 = $d_i^2/m_i + m_i$, 6 = $\sqrt{m_i^2 + 3d_i^2}$, 7 = $d_i^2/m_i + d_i$, 8 = $\sqrt{d_i(m_i + 3d_i)}$, 9 = $(m_i + 3d_i)/2$, 10 = $\sqrt{d_i(d_i + 3m_i)}$, 11 = $2m_i^3/d_i^2$, 12 = $\sqrt{2m_i^2 + 2d_i^2}$, 13 = $2m_i^4/d_i^3$, 14 = $2d_i^3/m_i^2$, 16 = $2d_i^4/m_i^3$, 18 = $\sqrt{2m_i^3/d_i + 2d_i^2}$, 19 = $(4d_i^4 + 12d_i m_i^3)^{1/4}$, 20 = $(1/2)\sqrt{7d_i^2 + 9m_i^2}$, 21 = $\sqrt{d_i^3/m_i + 3m_i^2}$, 22 = $(2d_i^4 + 14d_i^2m_i^2)^{1/4}$, 23 = $\sqrt{d_i^2 + 3d_i m_i}$, 24 = $(6d_i^4 + 10m_i^4)^{1/4}$, 25 = $(3d_i^4 + 13d_i^2m_i^2)^{1/4}$, 26 = $(1/2)\sqrt{5d_i^2 + 11d_i m_i}$, 27 = $\sqrt{(3d_i^2 + 5d_i m_i)/2}$, 30 = $m_i^3/d_i^2 + d_i^2/m_i$; and edge-type bounds 33 = $2(d_i + d_j) - (m_i + m_j)$, 34 = $2(d_i^2 + d_j^2)/(d_i + d_j)$, 35 = $2(d_i^2 + d_j^2)/(m_i + m_j)$, 37 = $\sqrt{2(d_i^2 + d_j^2)}$, 38 = $2 + \sqrt{2(d_i - 1)^2 + 2(d_j - 1)^2}$, 39 = $2 + \sqrt{2(d_i^2 + d_j^2) - 4(m_i + m_j) + 4}$, 40 = $2 + \sqrt{2((m_i - 1)^2 + (m_j - 1)^2) + (d_i^2 + d_j^2) - (d_i m_i + d_j m_j)}$, 42 = $\sqrt{d_i^2 + d_j^2 + 2m_i m_j}$, 47 = $(2(d_i^2 + d_j^2) - (m_i - m_j)^2)/(d_i + d_j)$, and 56 = $(d_i^2 + d_j^2)(m_i + m_j)/(2d_i d_j)$. Which statement gives exactly the candidates in this list that are universally valid upper bounds for $\mu(G)$, with the others failing for at least one connected graph?

## Choices
A. For every such graph $G$, the candidate inequalities numbered 1, 4–10, 12, 14, 16, 23, 25–27, 33–35, 37–39, and 42 hold, while each of 11, 13, 18–22, 24, 30, 40, 47, and 56 fails for some connected graph.
B. For every such graph $G$, the candidate inequalities numbered 1, 4–10, 12, 14, 16, 23, 25–27, 33–35, 37–39, 42, and 47 hold, while each of 11, 13, 18–22, 24, 30, 40, and 56 fails for some connected graph.
C. For every such graph $G$, the candidate inequalities numbered 1, 4–10, 12, 14, 16, 23, 25–27, 33–35, 37–40 hold, while each of 11, 13, 18–22, 24, 30, 42, 47, and 56 fails for some connected graph.
D. For every such graph $G$, the candidate inequalities numbered 1, 4–10, 12, 14, 16, 23, 24, 26–27, 33–35, 37–39, and 42 hold, while each of 11, 13, 18–22, 25, 30, 40, 47, and 56 fails for some connected graph.
E. For every such graph $G$, the candidate inequalities numbered 1, 4–10, 12, 14, 16, 23, 25–27, 33–35, 37–39, and 42 hold.
