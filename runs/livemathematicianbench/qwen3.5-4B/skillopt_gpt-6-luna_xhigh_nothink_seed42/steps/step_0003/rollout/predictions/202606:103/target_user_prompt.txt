## Question
Let $K$ be an alternating knot, and let $\Delta_K(t)=\sum_i a_i t^i$ denote its Alexander polynomial. A sequence $(b_i)$ is called trapezoidal if there exist indices $p\le q$ such that $b_i$ is weakly increasing up to $p$, constant for $p\le i\le q$, and weakly decreasing after $q$. Which of the following is the strongest valid general statement about the coefficient sequence $(|a_i|)$?

## Choices
A. The sequence $(|a_i|)$ is trapezoidal. Moreover, in the stronger variant, strict log-concavity is imposed on the rising and falling sides only when the maximal coefficient occurs on a plateau of positive length; if the maximum is attained at a single central index, the stronger condition adds no further inequalities.
B. The sequence $(|a_i|)$ is trapezoidal. Moreover, the strongest general refinement available is only asymptotic: along any infinite family of alternating knots, all sufficiently deep interior non-plateau indices are strictly log-concave, but finitely many such indices may fail for each knot.
C. The sequence $(|a_i|)$ is trapezoidal. Moreover, in the stronger variant, for every interior index $i$ belonging to a non-plateau portion of the sequence, the coefficients are strictly log-concave in the sense that $a_i^2 > |a_{i-1}|\,|a_{i+1}|$.
D. The sequence $(|a_i|)$ is trapezoidal. Moreover, in the stronger variant, for every interior index $i$, including indices lying in the constant plateau when one exists, the coefficients satisfy the strict inequality $a_i^2 > |a_{i-1}|\,|a_{i+1}|$.
E. The sequence $(|a_i|)$ is trapezoidal.
