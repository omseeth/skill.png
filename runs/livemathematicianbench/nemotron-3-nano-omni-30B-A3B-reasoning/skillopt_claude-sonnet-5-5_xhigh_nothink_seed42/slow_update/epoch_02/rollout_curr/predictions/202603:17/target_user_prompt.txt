## Question
Let \(V\) be a 4-dimensional vector space and let \(G=\operatorname{Gr}(2,\operatorname{Sym}^2 V^\vee)\), so that a point of \(G\) is a 2-dimensional subspace \(W=\langle Q_0,Q_1\rangle\subset \operatorname{Sym}^2 V^\vee\), equivalently a pencil of quadrics in \(\mathbb P(V)\cong \mathbb P^3\). Let \(\operatorname{PGL}(V)\) act naturally on \(G\), and let \(U_{\mathrm{sm}}\subset G\) be the open locus of pencils whose complete intersection \(\{Q_0=Q_1=0\}\subset \mathbb P(V)\) is smooth. For such a pencil, its discriminant binary quartic is \(\Delta_W(s,t)=\det(sQ_0+tQ_1)\), which has a classical \(j\)-invariant on the smooth locus. Which of the following is the strongest existence statement valid in this setting?

## Choices
A. There exists a rational map \(p\colon G \dashrightarrow \mathbb P^1\) whose restriction to \(U_{\mathrm{sm}}\) is exactly the \(j\)-invariant of the discriminant binary quartic.
B. There exists a rational \(\operatorname{PGL}(V)\)-invariant map \(p\colon G \dashrightarrow \mathbb P^1\) whose restriction to \(U_{\mathrm{sm}}\) is exactly the \(j\)-invariant of the discriminant binary quartic, and for which \(p^{-1}(\infty)\) is supported in codimension at least two.
C. There exists a rational \(\operatorname{PGL}(V)\)-invariant map \(p\colon G \dashrightarrow \mathbb P^1\) whose restriction to \(U_{\mathrm{sm}}\) is exactly the \(j\)-invariant of the discriminant binary quartic.
D. There exists a rational \(\operatorname{PGL}(V)\)-invariant map \(p\colon G \dashrightarrow \mathbb P^1\) whose restriction to \(U_{\mathrm{sm}}\) is exactly the \(j\)-invariant of the discriminant binary quartic, and such that for every finite value \(a\in \mathbb P^1\) in its image the reduced fiber \(\bigl(p^{-1}(a)\bigr)_{\mathrm{red}}\) is a single \(\operatorname{PGL}(V)\)-orbit.
E. There exists a regular \(\operatorname{PGL}(V)\)-invariant morphism \(p\colon G \to \mathbb P^1\) whose restriction to \(U_{\mathrm{sm}}\) is exactly the \(j\)-invariant of the discriminant binary quartic.
