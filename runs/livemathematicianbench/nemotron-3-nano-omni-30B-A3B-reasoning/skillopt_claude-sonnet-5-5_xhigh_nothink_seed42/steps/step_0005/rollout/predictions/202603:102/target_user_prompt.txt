## Question
Work in complex coordinates, with the fixed circumcircle taken to be the unit circle \(\mathbb T=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=1\}\). Let \(\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb T\) denote the set of unimodular parameters \(\lambda\) that actually correspond to triangles in a family \(T(\lambda)=A_1A_2A_3\), where each triangle is inscribed in \(\mathbb T\) and circumscribed about the same central conic (that is, each side of the triangle is tangent to a conic having a center). Let \(R(A_1,A_2,A_3)\) be any rational function of the vertex coordinates that is symmetric under every permutation of \(A_1,A_2,A_3\). Which of the following is the strongest statement that holds for every such \(R\)?

## Choices
A. Once \(R(A_1,A_2,A_3)\) has been written as a rational function of \(\lambda\) and of the coordinates of the two foci of the common central conic, that representation already shows that \(R\) is invariant throughout the family.
B. \(R(A_1,A_2,A_3)\) can always be expressed as a rational function of the parameter \(\lambda\) and of the coordinates of the two foci of the common central conic.
C. Whenever \(R(A_1,A_2,A_3)\) remains invariant throughout the family, its common value is a rational function of the coordinates of the two foci of the common central conic.
D. After restricting to the admissible parameter set \(\Lambda\), the dependence on \(\lambda\) can always be eliminated, so \(R(A_1,A_2,A_3)\) is a rational function of the coordinates of the two foci of the common central conic alone.
E. \(R(A_1,A_2,A_3)\) remains invariant throughout the family if and only if the corresponding rational function is constant for every unimodular parameter \(\lambda\in\mathbb T\) for which that rational expression is defined.
