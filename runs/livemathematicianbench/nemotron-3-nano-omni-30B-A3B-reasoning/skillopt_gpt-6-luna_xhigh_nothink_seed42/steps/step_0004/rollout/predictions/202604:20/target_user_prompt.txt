## Question
Let \(\Gamma\) be an arbitrary set, and let \(\ell_1(\Gamma)\) denote the Banach lattice of all scalar families \(x=(x_\gamma)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\) such that \(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|x_\gamma|<\infty\), equipped with its usual \(\ell_1\)-norm and pointwise order. Which statement gives the exact general validity of the semiprojectivity and \(1^+\)-projectivity properties for \(\ell_1(\Gamma)\) as \(\Gamma\) varies over all sets?

## Choices
A. For every countable set \(\Gamma\), the Banach lattice \(\ell_1(\Gamma)\) is semiprojective and \(1^+\)-projective.
B. The Banach lattice \(\ell_1(\Gamma)\) is semiprojective if and only if every pairwise disjoint family of nonzero positive vectors in \(\ell_1(\Gamma)\) is countable; equivalently, iff \(\Gamma\) is countable, and then it is also \(1^+\)-projective.
C. For every set \(\Gamma\), the Banach lattice \(\ell_1(\Gamma)\) is semiprojective, and it is \(1^+\)-projective if and only if \(\Gamma\) is countable.
D. For every set \(\Gamma\), the Banach lattice \(\ell_1(\Gamma)\) is semiprojective, and it is \(1^+\)-projective if and only if every lattice homomorphism from \(\ell_1(\Gamma)\) into a separable Banach lattice is nonzero on only finitely many coordinate vectors.
E. For every set \(\Gamma\), the Banach lattice \(\ell_1(\Gamma)\) is semiprojective, and in fact it is \(1^+\)-projective for every \(\Gamma\), because every lattice homomorphism from \(\ell_1(\Gamma)\) into a separable Banach lattice is nonzero on at most countably many coordinate vectors.
