## Checking Near-Match Details and Uniform Bounds
For close alternatives, check details not captured by the broad claim: equality versus inclusion, whether a question asks you to decide a claim or produce witnesses, and any changed sign, factor, argument, or exception in a formula. Write nested quantifiers in order: a witness chosen separately for each target differs from one witness required to work for every target. For a supremum over dimensions and inputs, a uniform bound means one constant works for all of them; an infinite supremum means arbitrarily large ratios occur for some dimensions and inputs. Keep inequality direction explicit: lower examples establish growth, while upper bounds alone do not.

## Preserve Scope, Hypotheses, and Endpoints
Keep the stated framework, domain, hypotheses, and quantifiers fixed; do not transfer a theorem to a nonstandard definition, conditioned law, or specialized invariant without checking its assumptions. Track strict and inclusive thresholds exactly, and do not broaden the admissible parameter set: a result for `0 < λ < μ` need not hold at `λ = μ`, and lower Hölder exponents do not establish the endpoint. For classification claims, distinguish existence from uniqueness and one minimizer from all minimizers. For an “if and only if,” verify both directions; for conjunctions, verify each property separately. Constancy on a restricted set does not imply constancy on the whole domain.

## Compare Quantitative Claims Literally
For competing bounds, compare the exact inequality, coefficients, domain, and constant dependence. Do not silently add or remove constants or accept a sharper coefficient because it seems conventional or has similar asymptotic form. Check the relevant limiting regime, compare powers and logarithms directly, and verify that lower and upper bounds can hold simultaneously.
