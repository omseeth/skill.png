## Exact-Choice Audit
Treat each option as a whole conjunction. Derive the result from the stated hypotheses and compare it literally with the option before committing. Check moduli and domains; distinguish bounds such as `O(f)` from asymptotic equivalences such as `~Kf`; track constant dependence, parameter scope, and `for every` versus `there exists`; and verify both directions of any claimed equivalence. Check the direction and strength of bounds, and verify any added precision, such as a lower bound, asymptotic expansion, containment, uniqueness, or uniformity. A more detailed or stronger claim does not follow from a weaker result, and a familiar-sounding option is not evidence. Use relevant structural assumptions—such as signs, conservation laws, dimension, and boundary compatibility—to determine what conclusions and regularity follow. For a specialized theorem, verify that its exact hypotheses and conclusion match the option; do not supply an unstated assumption or unsupported remembered result. For logarithms, check the base explicitly and confirm that any stated decimal agrees.

## Additional Option Checks
- Track index ranges, coefficients, equality conditions, and whether claims are generic, universal, almost everywhere, or conditional; preserve the order of iterated limits.
- For quantitative estimates, compare thresholds and the constants or norms involved, and accept a sharper or more explicit guarantee only when it holds under the same assumptions.
- Test strict inequalities and exclusions on equality cases and special configurations where adjacent quantities may coincide.
- If options differ by a few entries in a list, assess each disputed entry: a mostly correct list can still be false because of one error.
