## Question
Let \(\mathbb S^n\) denote the \(n\)-dimensional sphere. For integers \(p,q\ge 1\), call an embedding \(f:M\hookrightarrow \mathbb S^N\) minimal if its image is a minimal submanifold of \(\mathbb S^N\). What is the strongest statement that can be proved about the existence of minimal embeddings of \(\mathbb S^p\times \mathbb S^q\times \mathbb S^1\) under these assumptions?

## Choices
A. For every pair of integers \(p,q\ge 1\), there exists a minimal embedding \(f:\mathbb S^p\times \mathbb S^q\times \mathbb S^1\hookrightarrow \mathbb S^{p+q+2}\) whose image is contained in some totally geodesic equatorial \(\mathbb S^{p+q+1}\subset \mathbb S^{p+q+2}\).
B. For every pair of integers \(p,q\ge 1\), there exists some integer \(N=N(p,q)\) and a minimal embedding \(f:\mathbb S^p\times \mathbb S^q\times \mathbb S^1\hookrightarrow \mathbb S^{N}\).
C. For every pair of integers \(p,q\ge 1\), there exists a minimal embedding \(f:\mathbb S^p\times \mathbb S^q\times \mathbb S^1\hookrightarrow \mathbb S^{p+q+2}\) obtained by reflecting across an equatorial \(\mathbb S^{p+q+1}\) a smooth minimal hypersurface that meets that equator at a constant angle \(\theta\in(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2})\).
D. For every pair of integers \(p,q\ge 1\), there exists a minimal embedding \(f:\mathbb S^p\times \mathbb S^q\times \mathbb S^1\hookrightarrow \mathbb S^{p+q+2}\).
E. For every pair of integers \(p,q\ge 1\), there exists a minimal embedding \(f:\mathbb S^p\times \mathbb S^q\times \mathbb S^1\hookrightarrow \mathbb S^{p+q+1}\).
