## Question
Let $P$ be a pencil (that is, a one-dimensional linear system) of plane curves of degree $d>2$ in $\mathbb{CP}^2$. Assume its base locus (the common intersection of all members of the pencil) consists of simple base points, meaning each base point has multiplicity $1$. For a fixed integer $k$, let $m_k$ denote the number of reducible curves in $P$ whose irreducible components all have degree at most $k$. Which of the following is the strongest valid quantitative estimate for $m_k$ under these assumptions?

## Choices
A. One has $m_k\le 6$ whenever $d>2k$; equivalently, the explicit large-degree hypothesis can be replaced by the simpler condition that the degree exceed twice the maximal allowed component degree.
B. For each fixed $k$, there exists a threshold $D(k)$ such that every pencil as above with $d\ge D(k)$ satisfies $m_k\le 6$; in particular, $m_k\le 6$ for all sufficiently large $d$.
C. One has $m_k\le 6$ whenever $d\ge \tfrac{7}{2}k(k-1)-2$; equivalently, for sufficiently large $d$, the number of such reducible curves in the pencil cannot exceed $6$.
D. One has $m_k\le 6$ whenever $d\ge \tfrac{7}{2}k(k-1)-2$, and this same estimate continues to hold even if the base locus is allowed to have non-simple base points.
E. One has $m_k\le 5$ whenever $d\ge \tfrac{7}{2}k(k-1)-2$; equivalently, for sufficiently large $d$ a pencil cannot contain as many as six reducible members whose components all have degree at most $k$.
