## Question
Let \(\Gamma\) be a non-uniform arithmetic lattice in \(\mathrm{SO}^+(n,1)\), the identity component of the isometry group of hyperbolic \(n\)-space, with \(n\ge 4\). Here non-uniform means that \(\Gamma\) acts discretely with finite covolume but noncompact quotient. A surface subgroup of \(\Gamma\) means a subgroup isomorphic to the fundamental group of a closed surface of genus \(g\ge 2\), and a thin surface subgroup means such a subgroup that has infinite index in \(\Gamma\) and is Zariski dense in \(\mathrm{SO}^+(n,1)\). Which of the following is the strongest existence statement valid in this setting?

## Choices
A. \(\Gamma\) contains a subgroup isomorphic to the fundamental group of a closed surface of genus at least \(2\), without asserting thinness.
B. \(\Gamma\) contains a thin surface subgroup that has finite index in a subgroup of \(\Gamma\) isomorphic to the fundamental group of a finite-volume hyperbolic \(3\)-manifold.
C. \(\Gamma\) contains a thin surface subgroup that is contained in a subgroup of \(\Gamma\) commensurable with a finite-volume arithmetic lattice in \(\mathrm{SO}^+(3,1)\).
D. \(\Gamma\) contains a thin surface subgroup.
E. \(\Gamma\) contains a thin surface subgroup that can always be chosen to come from a totally geodesic immersed closed hyperbolic surface in \(\mathbb H^n/\Gamma\).
