## Question
Let $K$ be a genus-one knot with Seifert matrix $M=\begin{pmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} \\ a_{21} & a_{22} \end{pmatrix}$ in the canonical band basis (the standard basis coming from the two bands of a genus-one Seifert surface), and for a nonzero integer $\ell$ let $K(\ell,0)$ denote the knot obtained by adding $2\ell$ twists to the first band. Here, $K$ and $K(\ell,0)$ are called $S$-equivalent if they admit Seifert matrices related by the usual $S$-equivalence on Seifert matrices (generated by integral unimodular congruence and elementary expansions/reductions). Which description gives the complete classification of when $K$ and $K(\ell,0)$ are $S$-equivalent?

## Choices
A. $K$ and $K(\ell,0)$ are $S$-equivalent precisely for those cases in which $a_{22}=0$ and $a_{12}+a_{21}$ divides $2\ell$.
B. $K$ and $K(\ell,0)$ are $S$-equivalent precisely for those cases in which $a_{22}=0$ and $\ell$ divides $a_{12}+a_{21}$.
C. $K$ and $K(\ell,0)$ are $S$-equivalent precisely for those cases in which $a_{22}=0$ and $a_{12}+a_{21}$ divides $\ell$.
D. $K$ and $K(\ell,0)$ are $S$-equivalent precisely for those cases in which $a_{22}=0$; the divisibility condition on $a_{12}+a_{21}$ is needed only for unimodular congruence, not for full $S$-equivalence.
E. Whenever $K$ and $K(\ell,0)$ are $S$-equivalent, one must have $a_{22}=0$.
