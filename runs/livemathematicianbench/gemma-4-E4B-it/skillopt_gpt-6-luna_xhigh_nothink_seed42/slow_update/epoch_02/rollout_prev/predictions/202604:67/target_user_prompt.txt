## Question
Let $S$ be a compact Hausdorff topological Clifford semigroup. Write $E(S)$ for the set of idempotents of $S$, viewed as its semilattice of idempotents, and for each $e\in E(S)$ let $G_e$ denote the maximal subgroup of $S$ with identity $e$. Assume that $E(S)$ is a metrizable perfect semilattice, that each $G_e$ is compact and metrizable, and that the associated functor $e\mapsto G_e$ preserves inverse limits. Here, the Bowman topology means the canonical topology on $S$ arising from the Bowman construction associated with $E(S)$ and the family $(G_e)_{e\in E(S)}$. Which of the following is the strongest conclusion that holds about $S$ with its Bowman topology?

## Choices
A. The Bowman topology on $S$ is metrizable.
B. It is possible to construct an explicit metric on $S$ whose induced topology is exactly the Bowman topology, and this metric can always be obtained from a metric on $E(S)$ together with only finitely many weighted projection terms coming from lower groups.
C. It is possible to construct an explicit metric on $S$ whose induced topology is exactly the Bowman topology using only a compatible metric on $E(S)$, without any auxiliary metrics coming from the maximal subgroups.
D. The original compact Hausdorff topology already carried by $S$ must coincide with the Bowman topology; hence one automatically obtains an explicit metric compatible with the original topology of $S$.
E. It is possible to construct an explicit metric on $S$ whose induced topology is exactly the Bowman topology.
