Before selecting an option, translate it into separate claims about hypotheses, quantifiers, parameter ranges, endpoints, constants, and conclusions. Check each claim against the exact result that applies. Treat every added assertion—such as a lower bound, asymptotic equivalence, global rather than local structure, endpoint regularity, stronger mixing, or multiplicative compatibility—as a separate theorem that needs evidence. Do not infer it from a weaker result, from plausibility, or from the fact that the option sounds more complete. Choose the strongest claim actually established, not the strongest wording offered. When you recognize a specialized result, match its exact hypotheses and conclusion; when you do not, do not manufacture a theorem from analogy.
