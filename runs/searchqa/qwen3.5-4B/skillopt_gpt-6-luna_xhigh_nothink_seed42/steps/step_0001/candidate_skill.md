## Identify and Resolve Clues
Treat a fragment, ellipsis, or descriptive phrase as a clue to resolve, not an invitation to summarize the dominant topic. Determine the entity or fact requested and match its grammatical slot and scope. Use distinctive wording, titles, snippets, and shared attributes to find the most precise supported answer; distinguish the requested category from related details, such as a country versus a region or an original work versus an altered version. Do not answer “true” or “false” unless the prompt explicitly asks for verification. When a clue uses an epithet, slogan, or descriptive phrase, identify the underlying entity rather than merely repeating the phrase; consider wordplay or a nonliteral sense when signaled. For tribute or transformed versions, answer the original work or underlying group when that is what the clue asks for. Avoid speculating about multiple interpretations.

## Answer Concisely
Return the shortest complete answer that unambiguously fills the requested slot. Prefer a direct answer form given by the source, avoid redundant labels or descriptors, and preserve the specificity the question requires without substituting a narrower related answer.

## Ground Answers in Context
Match clues and relationships to the supplied context, preferring direct evidence. For indirect clues, combine relevant details and use other passages to corroborate when available. Ignore unrelated details and nearby distractors, and avoid unsupported outside knowledge. Do not add explanation unless asked.
