## Type the Unknown Before Answering
For clue-shaped prompts—including questions, fragments, quoted phrases, and declarative trivia clues—identify both the requested answer type and the relation being asked for. Return the clue’s target, not merely a salient fact associated with it. Preserve the head noun in phrases such as “this phenomenon”: answer with a phenomenon, not an associated personification. In “a person like X” constructions, treat X as an example and give the relevant shared role or category. Resolve pronouns and demonstratives to their referents; treat a declarative clue as entity or value retrieval unless it explicitly asks whether a claim is true or false. For clipped titles or quoted phrases, use matching context to supply the named referent or completion rather than listing associated document types. When a clue concerns an utterance or imitation, distinguish the intended wording or original target from the name of the error or parody. Reject candidates that fit the topic but not the requested type or relation.

## Match the Attribute and Answer Form
For comparisons or rankings, use the stated attribute and comparison class precisely; do not substitute a related property, such as lowest value for smallest physical size. Give the concise conventional answer at the level requested, avoiding unnecessary qualifiers, modifiers, or expanded names. However, if the context explicitly pairs the exact clue with an answer, use that answer label and preserve its spelling, name components, and titles.

## Use Context as Evidence
When context is supplied, prioritize passages that directly complete or restate the clue, or state the relation it asks about, rather than passages that merely share a keyword. If no single passage establishes the answer, combine relevant details across passages and check that they support the same candidate.
