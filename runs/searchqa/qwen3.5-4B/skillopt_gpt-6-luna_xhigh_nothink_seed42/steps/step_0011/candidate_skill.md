## Type the Unknown Before Answering
Before retrieving, paraphrase what the clue asks for as a type of answer, such as a person, place, object, ingredient, or phenomenon. Preserve the head noun in phrases such as “this phenomenon”: answer with a phenomenon, not an associated personification. In “a person like X” constructions, treat X as an example and give the relevant shared role or category. An epithet or description usually points to the named entity it describes; an ingredient is not the dish made from it, and a look-alike is not necessarily a specialized kind of double. For pronouns and demonstratives, resolve the referent; do not mistake a declarative clue for a request to judge its truth. Check that a candidate fits the clue’s distinctive details and relationships together, not just an associated topic or name, and reject candidates that are not the requested kind of thing.

## Anchor Time-Sensitive Clues
Interpret words such as “current,” “former,” and “now” relative to the clue’s own source or reference date, which may differ from today. Use distinctive biographical or historical details to disambiguate candidates rather than relying on a present-day title alone.

## Handle Elliptical Clues
When a prompt is only a fragment and has no explicit question, infer the most salient answer relation or shared category from the named entities and clue context. Do not mistake the dominant topic of retrieved passages for the requested answer, and do not add a relation the prompt does not support.

## Use Canonical Answer Forms
For short-answer tasks, give the concise conventional form supported by the context. Use the shortest canonical label that identifies the requested answer without adding generic words or explanatory wording. Preserve meaningful parts of proper names, including customary titles when relevant, and use the singular headword when the clue asks for a term even if its description is plural.

## Cross-Check Contextual Evidence
When relevant passages or context are available, prefer direct evidence and cross-check them before settling on an answer.
